Puerto Viejo de Talamanca, Costa Rica

Discover Puerto Viejo

Where Caribbean culture, tropical nature and ocean life meet.

Puerto Viejo de Talamanca is more than a beach destination. It's a small Caribbean town shaped by Afro-Caribbean culture, tropical forest, beaches, surfing, local food and a slower way of life. This page is our answer to the question most visitors ask before they arrive: what is it actually like here?

Chapter 01 — The Caribbean

The Caribbean Soul of Puerto Viejo

Puerto Viejo's identity is deeply connected to the Caribbean. Many families here descend from Jamaican and other West Indian workers who came to Costa Rica's Limón province in the late 1800s to build the railroad and work the banana farms. That heritage still shapes the town's food, music, language and pace of daily life.

You'll hear it in calypso and reggae drifting out of open-air bars, and in Limonese Creole, an English-based language still spoken between neighbors alongside Spanish. The Bribri and Cabécar Indigenous communities of the Talamanca mountains are close neighbors too, which is why cacao, jungle medicine and river life are woven so deeply into the region.

Language
Spanish, English and Limonese Creole — often in the same conversation.
Music
Calypso limonense, reggae and dancehall, from beach bars to Saturday nights.
Pace
Bicycles, open doors and "pura vida" taken seriously.

Chapter 02 — Food & Fruits

Flavors of the Caribbean

A taste of Puerto Viejo's tropical side.

Caribbean cooking here runs on coconut, allspice, thyme and Scotch bonnet peppers — a very different table from the rest of Costa Rica. A few things worth trying:

  • Rice and beans

    Cooked in coconut milk with thyme and a whole hot pepper; the weekend staple, often with Caribbean chicken.

  • Rondón

    A slow coconut stew of fish or seafood with root vegetables and plantain. The name comes from "run down."

  • Patí

    A flaky turnover filled with spiced beef and a little heat, sold warm from bakeries and street carts.

  • Pan bon

    A dark, sweet Caribbean bread with cheese and candied fruit, especially around the holidays.

  • Agua de sapo

    Ginger, lime and raw cane sugar over ice — the local answer to the humidity.

What grows around Puerto Viejo?

Heat, rain and rich soil make the Caribbean coast one of the best places in Costa Rica to taste tropical fruit straight from the tree. Many of these grow on farms and in gardens around Punta Uva, including on our own property.

  • Cacao

    Cacao

    The fruit behind chocolate, grown in Talamanca for centuries. The sweet white pulp around the beans is a treat on its own.

  • Rambutan

    Mamón chino

    Spiky red shell, juicy grape-like fruit inside. Sold by the bunch at roadside stands in season.

  • Mangosteen

    Mangostán

    A deep purple shell around soft white segments: sweet, floral and a little tart.

  • Snake fruit

    Salak

    Scaly brown skin that peels like a shell, with crisp, sweet-tart flesh. A rare find from the area’s exotic-fruit farms.

  • Jackfruit

    Jaca

    The largest tree fruit in the world, sweet and fragrant when ripe.

  • Breadfruit

    Fruta de pan

    A starchy Caribbean staple, fried, roasted or boiled like a potato.

  • Coconut

    Pipa

    Drunk straight from the green nut, and the base of almost every Caribbean dish.

  • Bananas

    Banano

    Limón is Costa Rica’s banana heartland. Sweet ones for breakfast, green ones and plantains fried into patacones.

  • Taro

    Ñampí

    A hairy, ringed root with pale purple flesh, cooked into Caribbean soups like rondón.

  • Zapote

    Mamey sapote

    Rough brown skin around sweet, creamy, salmon-colored flesh, often blended into a batido.

  • Custard apple

    Anona

    Soft white flesh as sweet as custard, with large black seeds. Eat it with a spoon.

  • Dragon fruit

    Pitahaya

    Bright pink skin and magenta flesh speckled with seeds; mild, sweet and very refreshing.

  • Passion fruit

    Maracuyá

    Tart, fragrant pulp scooped straight from the shell or blended into a fresco.

  • Papaya

    Papaya

    Sweet orange flesh, eaten at breakfast all over Costa Rica with a squeeze of lime.

  • Limes

    Limón

    Squeezed over everything from papaya to ceviche; plenty of yards here have their own tree.

  • Durian

    Durián

    Spiky and famously pungent, with rich, custard-like flesh. Grown by a few exotic-fruit farms on this coast.

  • Pineapple

    Piña

    Costa Rica is one of the world’s biggest pineapple growers; a ripe one here tastes nothing like the export kind.

Chapter 03 — Beaches

The Beaches of Puerto Viejo

A single coastal road runs south from Puerto Viejo to Manzanillo, and almost every turn off it leads to a different beach. Each one has its own character:

  • Photo coming soon

    Playa Negra

    Dark volcanic sand just north of town, and a completely different atmosphere from the beaches to the south.

  • Playa Cocles

    Surf, palm trees and lifeguards; one of the most recognizable beaches in the area.

  • Playa Chiquita

    A quieter stretch of coastline with tide pools, framed by tropical vegetation.

  • Our beach

    Punta Uva

    Jungle, turquoise water and calm, swimmable coves — named among the world’s 50 best beaches in 2025.

    Explore our villas

  • Manzanillo

    The end of the road, where the coast becomes the Gandoca-Manzanillo Wildlife Refuge.

Life revolves around the ocean

Days here tend to organize themselves around the water: an early swim before the heat, surf when the swell comes in, snorkeling on the reef when the sea is calm, and everyone drifting back to the sand for sunset. Beaches are where locals and visitors actually meet.

Chapter 04 — Jungle & Wildlife

Where the Jungle Meets the Sea

One of the things that makes the Caribbean coast different is how close nature is to everyday life. Around Puerto Viejo and Punta Uva, the jungle surrounds the roads, homes and beaches, and wildlife is often found surprisingly close to town — a sloth above the bike path, howler monkeys as your alarm clock, a frog the size of a fingernail on the porch.

Chapter 05 — Staying Longer

What Is It Like to Live in Puerto Viejo?

Thinking about staying longer?

Puerto Viejo has a rhythm that's easy to fall into. Mornings at the beach, afternoons exploring the jungle, local food, bicycles instead of cars and a community that attracts people from around the world.

A few things people consider before staying longer

Internet
Fiber-optic internet is available in much of the area; our villas also have Starlink backup for remote work.
Groceries
Small markets near Punta Uva cover the basics; larger supermarkets and a weekly farmers market are in Puerto Viejo.
Getting around
Bicycles, tuk-tuks, taxis and local buses. The main road is paved, so no 4x4 is needed.
Healthcare
Puerto Viejo has clinics and pharmacies; the nearest full hospital is in Limón city.
Weather
Warm year-round. It can rain any month, but September and October are often among the sunniest, calmest months here.
Nearby towns
Cahuita and its national park to the north; Manzanillo and the wildlife refuge to the south; Bribri inland.
